Approach

How I think about product.

Every new product, strategy, or vision is a bet placed with the company's money, whether the idea comes from the CEO, a customer, or my own team. Product's job is to improve the odds before that money is committed. That means researching the claim, testing it as cheaply as possible, and deciding where it belongs against everything else on the roadmap. Then it means measuring whether the bet paid off, in numbers the customer recognizes.

That's why I prototype with AI before asking engineering for a quarter. A working interaction gives customers and teams something specific to react to, and a cheap test is the best odds improvement there is. It's also why I tie product work to the metrics customers bring to their renewals.

I also see product as the bridge between engineering, marketing, and sales. I find a close leadership partner in each function, because most delays live in the gaps between teams. When we have something valuable, product should never be the reason a customer doesn't have it yet.

Engineer by training

I earned a B.S. in Mechanical Engineering / Mechanical Technology from Central Connecticut State University. Engineering taught me that most problems are solvable once you understand the system, have the right resources, and have room to make decisions. I bring that to product strategy, pricing and packaging, and prioritization: find how the parts interact, test the assumption most likely to break, and connect the work to what the business is worth.
